# Waterline: video narration (about 2:40)

Read it slowly and naturally, in your own voice. Change any words that don't sound like you. Leave a short pause at each line break. Record in QuickTime (File → New Audio Recording), in a quiet room, phone face down.

---

**[0:00–0:12, the water rising through the living room]**
This is where a hundred and fifty centimetres of floodwater reaches in a living room.
Up to my chin.

**[0:12–0:35, the start, Bentong facts on screen]**
I'm Shermaine, from Bentong, Pahang.
In December 2021 the floods came at night. One family in Kampung Kemansur had water about one and a half metres deep inside their house.
When the river alert comes, one person moves everything, with minutes to spare and both hands full.
And nobody can picture where one and a half metres actually reaches.
So I made Waterline, for SPECS.

**[0:35–1:35, one uncut run at Danger]**
On a dry day, I look down and pinch the things I'd save. The plug, the school bag, the rice cooker. The photo album and my IC folder on the sideboard.
Then the river alert. Three levels, named after the real Sungai Bentong gauge.
I press Danger.
The water rises to its real height, against my body and my furniture.
A ring sits on the water under my head and follows me. The tag reads it off my body: up to my chin.
If I crouch, my eyes go under, and everything goes cool and muffled.
Only one thing glows: the lowest thing still under the water.
I grab it, carry it up, and let go near its pin. It snaps and turns green, and the next one lights up.
When something is out of view, an arrow and a splash show me where.
Everything is safe. I switch off the mains.
The water drains, the rain turns into morning birds, and the Lens gets out of the way.

**[1:35–2:00, Alert, the level card again]**
Each level is a different list. Alert flags two things, Warning three, Danger all five.
So I can rehearse the level the radio is warning about, before it happens.

**[2:00–2:25, the brief, the design rounds, LEAF table]**
I planned it with the plan-lens skill, and CLAD built it in Lens Studio.
Then I fixed it with design notes, not code, over four rounds.
The first room didn't look like a home, so I made it one.
The first seconds showed everything at once, so now it starts with one line.
And a full playthrough test found four moments with no cue, so now every step has one.
All six LEAF tests pass. The water reaches a hundred and fifty centimetres in under four seconds, and the underwater view switches on in under a tenth of a second.

**[2:25–2:40, What is simulated, end card]**
The room, my belongings and the levels are stand-ins. How it feels to lift a real rice cooker needs real glasses.
But the water, the depth on your body and the next thing to lift are built for real.
Waterline. See the water before it comes.
